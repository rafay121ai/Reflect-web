6 Rosebud Alternatives in 2026 (After the Free Plan Ended)

By iReflect Editorial Team. Published October 13, 2026. Last checked October 13, 2026.

Rosebud retired its free plan on September 30, 2026. If you journaled there for free, you now have three choices: pay for Rosebud Bloom, export your entries and move, or keep a second, cheaper tool for everyday writing.

This page compares six alternatives on what actually matters when you switch: how the AI responds, what it costs, where it runs, and what each company says about your data. Every fact about another app was checked against that app's own website on the date above. Prices change, so confirm before you subscribe.

Disclosure: we make iReflect, which is one of the six apps below. We have tried to describe it as plainly as the others, including what it does not do. iReflect is still in early access through a waitlist.

Quick answer

  • Closest free replacement: Reflection. Its free plan includes unlimited entries and an AI coach.
  • Deepest analysis: Mindsera. Thinking frameworks, emotion trends and voice conversations on its paid plan.
  • Talking with a mentor voice: Life Note. AI mentors modeled on historical figures.
  • Privacy first, AI optional: Day One. End to end encryption on every plan, AI only on Gold.
  • Free daily check-ins and mood tracking: Stoic.
  • Short guided reflection instead of long journaling: iReflect.

What changed at Rosebud

Rosebud's help center says the free plan was retired effective September 30, 2026, because the company moved everyone to a new memory system it cannot sustainably offer for free. It also says nothing you have written is being removed and that you can export your full journal at any time from the entry history page (source).

Rosebud Bloom is listed at $12.99 a month or $107.99 a year, and Rosebud mentions student and disability discounts. It runs on iOS, Android and the web.

What Rosebud still does well

Before you leave, be clear about what you would give up. Rosebud combines several things in one place: voice journaling, personalized prompts, weekly reports on your progress, guided journals such as an Internal Family Systems (IFS) journal, and habit and goal tools. It says entries are encrypted in transit and at rest, and its memory of past entries is the feature it is now charging for.

If you rely on long term memory across months of entries plus habits and goals in one app, none of the alternatives below copies that whole package exactly. Paying for Bloom may be the simplest answer. If you mainly want a place to write and a thoughtful reply, you have cheaper options.

1. Reflection

Reflection is the most direct swap if the free plan was the reason you used Rosebud. Its Free Forever plan includes unlimited private entries, guided monthly and annual reviews, prompts, reminders, import and export, and its AI coach, which asks follow up questions based on what you wrote. Premium costs $8 a month or $5.75 a month billed yearly and adds unlimited AI search and writing support, multiple photos per entry and unlimited sync.

Privacy: Reflection says entries are encrypted at rest with AES-256, are not used to train AI models on any plan, and that its AI runs on Google Gemini. You can turn the AI off with one toggle.

Where Rosebud is better: Reflection is a journal with an AI coach added, not an AI-first conversation. If you liked Rosebud's back and forth and its guided therapeutic journals, Reflection will feel quieter.

Best for: people who want a free, full featured journal with some AI help.

2. Mindsera

Mindsera calls itself a copilot for thinking. It offers 52 or more templates built on mental models such as First Principles Thinking, Ikigai and Regret Minimization, plus per entry analysis, long term emotion trends, recurring topic detection and a Big Five personality profile. Voice Mode transcribes spoken entries and Call Mode lets you talk back and forth.

Price: the free Curious plan covers basic journaling, habit tracking, AI prompts and framework templates. Genius, which includes voice and the full analysis, is $14.99 a month or $129 a year. That is slightly more than Rosebud monthly and more per year.

Privacy: Mindsera says entries are encrypted at rest and in transit, are not used to train AI models, and can be exported as Markdown or PDF.

Where Rosebud is better: Mindsera leans toward analysis and decision making. If you used Rosebud mainly for emotional support, its tone may feel more like a coach than a listener.

Best for: people who journal to think through decisions and like structure and data.

3. Life Note

Life Note pairs your entries with AI mentors modeled on historical figures, from Marcus Aurelius to Carl Jung. It lists more than 1,000 mentors, says replies draw on the figures' own writings, and has a Connect the Dots feature that links new entries to past ones. It runs on iOS, Android and the web.

Price: there is a free tier, and a Premium tier for a deeper experience. Its homepage does not list Premium prices, so check in the app.

Privacy: Life Note says entries are encrypted with AES-GCM and that you can export or delete your data at any time.

Where Rosebud is better: the mentor format is a matter of taste. Some people find a Stoic or a psychologist persona helpful; others prefer a plain reply that does not speak in someone else's voice.

Best for: people who enjoy philosophy and want a perspective outside their own.

4. Day One

Day One is a long established journal rather than an AI app, which is exactly why some people move to it. Its free Basic plan includes unlimited text entries and journals, daily prompts, search and end to end encryption, limited to one device. Silver ($49.99 a year) adds media, audio with transcription and unlimited devices. Gold ($74.99 a year) adds AI features: Daily Chat, reflective summaries and prompts, Go Deeper prompts and entry summaries, with daily usage limits.

Privacy: end to end encryption is included on every plan.

Where Rosebud is better: Day One's AI is an add on with usage limits, not the center of the app. If an AI conversation is what you came for, you will need Gold and it will still feel lighter.

Best for: people who want a durable, private record of their life and only occasional AI help.

5. Stoic

Stoic is built around routine: morning check-ins, evening wrap-ups, mood trends, guided journals with prompts it says come from therapists and experts, plus meditation and breathing exercises. It offers AI personalized prompts, and also uses streaks and badges. It runs on iPhone, iPad, Mac, Android and the web.

Price: its website describes Stoic as free and shows no paid tier. Check the app store listing for any optional upgrades.

Privacy: Stoic says journals are stored on your devices and backed up, and that it does not sell data. Its site does not explain the encryption used.

Where Rosebud is better: Stoic's AI personalizes prompts but is not a conversation partner in the way Rosebud is.

Best for: people who want a free daily habit with mood tracking.

6. iReflect

iReflect is not a journal in the usual sense. You start with whatever is on your mind, answer a few gentle questions that look at it from different angles, and get a short reflection back: a mirror of what you said, not advice or analysis. You can note your mood if you want. There are no streaks, scores or goals, and a weekly insight letter pulls things together.

Price: a 7 day free trial, then $7.99 a month or $71.99 a year. There is no free plan after the trial. iReflect is currently in early access through a waitlist, so it is not yet a drop in replacement you can switch to today.

Privacy: iReflect says reflections are stored encrypted, are never shared, sold or used to train AI, and that you can request full deletion at any time.

Where Rosebud is better: almost everywhere on features. iReflect has no voice journaling, no habit or goal tracking, no therapy framework journals and no long form writing space. It does one thing: help you look at a single thought clearly in a few minutes.

Best for: people who found long journaling sessions tiring and want something short that ends with clarity rather than a page of text. See what AI self-reflection is for how this differs from an AI journal.

How to choose

Most people switching from Rosebud care about one of four things. Decide which one first, then the choice is usually obvious.

  • Cost. If you want to keep paying nothing, look at Reflection, Stoic, Day One Basic or the free tiers of Mindsera and Life Note. Check what the AI does on each free plan before you move months of entries.
  • How the AI talks to you. Rosebud felt like a conversation. Mindsera analyzes, Life Note speaks as a mentor, Reflection asks follow up questions, iReflect mirrors. Try two for a week and notice which leaves you clearer rather than just more informed.
  • Privacy. Day One offers end to end encryption on every plan. Reflection and Mindsera state that entries are not used for model training. Read our AI journaling privacy checklist before sharing anything sensitive with any of them.
  • Memory. If you want an AI that remembers months of entries, that is the feature Rosebud now charges for, and it is the hardest to replace. Ask whether you need it, or whether a fresh reply each time is enough.

For a broader framework that is not tied to Rosebud, see how to choose a self-reflection app and our guide to AI journaling apps.

Before you switch: take your journal with you

Rosebud says you can export your full journal at any time from the entry history page. Do that first, even if you are not sure you are leaving. Then check whether the app you are moving to imports files. Reflection lists import and export on its free plan, and Mindsera exports Markdown and PDF, which is useful if you move again later.

Read through a few old entries before you import everything. A switch is a good moment to notice what you were actually writing about and what you wanted from the replies.

AI journaling apps, including iReflect, are not a substitute for professional help. If you are struggling, talk to a qualified professional or someone you trust, and contact local emergency services if you are in crisis.

FAQ

Is Rosebud still free?

No. Rosebud's help center says the free plan was retired on September 30, 2026. Your existing entries are kept and can be exported.

What is the best free alternative to Rosebud?

Reflection is the closest free match, because its free plan includes unlimited entries and its AI coach. Stoic is free if you want daily check-ins and mood tracking, and Day One Basic is free if privacy matters more than AI.

Which Rosebud alternative has the strongest privacy?

Day One includes end to end encryption on all plans. Among the AI-first apps, Reflection and Mindsera both state that entries are not used to train AI models. Read each privacy policy yourself before sharing sensitive details.

Can I move my Rosebud entries to another app?

You can export your full Rosebud journal from the entry history page. Whether you can import it depends on the destination app, so check its import options first.

Is an AI journal the same as therapy?

No. AI journaling can support everyday reflection but does not replace professional assessment, treatment or crisis care. See AI tools and therapy for where that line sits.
