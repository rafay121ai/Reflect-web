# iReflect Article SEO Rules

These rules are mandatory whenever creating, expanding, merging, or publishing blog articles in this repository, unless the user explicitly changes them.

## 1. Evidence before volume

- Review the latest available Google Search Console exports before selecting topics.
- Distinguish branded from non-branded demand and small samples from reliable signals.
- Do not claim guaranteed rankings, traffic, or a percentage likelihood unsupported by data.
- Prefer improving an existing page when it already satisfies the same search intent.

## 2. Prevent cannibalization

- Inventory existing titles, slugs, H1s, and intended queries before proposing a topic.
- Treat close query variants as one search-intent family unless the results demonstrate meaningfully different intent.
- Give every new article a unique primary intent, audience need, angle, and role in a topic cluster.

## 3. Require a content brief

Define before drafting:

- primary search intent and query family;
- user problem and direct answer;
- unique iReflect angle;
- outline and evidence requirements;
- related internal links;
- product-relevant, low-pressure CTA;
- success metric and review date.

## 4. Meet the quality bar

- Write for people first, with a useful answer near the beginning.
- Use original examples, practical reflection prompts, and enough depth to satisfy the intent without padding.
- Use primary or authoritative sources for health, psychology, privacy, product, or research claims.
- Avoid diagnosis, treatment promises, invented statistics, keyword stuffing, generic filler, and unsupported superlatives.
- Preserve iReflect's calm, non-prescriptive voice and its boundary that reflection is not therapy.

## 5. Complete on-page and technical SEO

Each article must have:

- a unique title, meta description, canonical URL, H1, and logical heading structure;
- index/follow directives, social metadata, and Article structured data where appropriate;
- accurate publication and modification dates;
- descriptive internal links to relevant cluster pages and at least one useful path toward the product;
- valid HTML, a 200 response after deployment, no accidental redirect or `noindex`, and inclusion in the blog index and sitemap.

## 6. Publish through the full workflow

Creation is not publication. For an authorized release:

1. create or update the files;
2. update internal links, blog index, and sitemap;
3. validate HTML, links, canonicals, and sitemap paths;
4. commit and push only intended files;
5. verify the production URL and live sitemap;
6. use Search Console for discovery and diagnosis, without treating sitemap submission as guaranteed indexing.

## 7. Measure and improve

- Record the publication date and evaluate meaningful trends over weeks, not hours.
- Monitor discovery, indexing, non-brand impressions, query families, position, CTR, and conversions.
- Expand winners, improve weak snippets, merge overlapping pages, or stop publishing into unsupported clusters based on evidence.
- Do not recommend another large batch while the previous batch is too new to evaluate, unless the user knowingly overrides that constraint.
